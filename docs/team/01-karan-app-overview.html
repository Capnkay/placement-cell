3. The two minute explanation

What it is. Every placement season a college has to match students to company drives. This app is that matching desk. It has two kinds of user: students and the placement officer.

Student side. A student registers with a real email and their academic record: branch, CGPA, backlogs. From then on the app compares that record with the rules on every drive. Drives they qualify for show an Apply button. Drives they do not qualify for show the exact reason, for example "minimum CGPA is 7.5, yours is 6.4". They apply, and then follow the status and the officer's notes.

Officer side. The officer maintains the companies, announces drives with their rules, moves candidates from applied to shortlisted to interview to selected, and can shortlist many at once. There are reports that come straight from the database with plain JDBC, a page that shows the database itself, and an audit trail of everything that changed.

Behind it. Servlets receive requests, EJB beans hold the business rules, JPA and Hibernate save the objects to MySQL, and JSP pages draw the screens. The reports use JDBC directly on the same connection pool. Each of us will now explain our own layer.

4. The story, step by step

A student's journey

  1. Register at /register. Name, college email, password, roll number, branch, year, CGPA, backlogs, phone. The row goes to app_user and student_profile. (Tiya explains how.)
  2. Sign in at /login. The servlet checks the password hash and stores a small SessionUser in the HttpSession.
  3. Dashboard at /app/dashboard: profile summary, counts of your applications by status, how many open drives you can still apply to, and a note if the resume or phone number is missing.
  4. Drives board at /app/drives. DriveBrowseServlet asks DriveService.check for each open drive and shows the verdict.
  5. Apply: open a drive, press Apply. ApplicationServlet.doPost checks the rules again, then ApplicationService.apply writes the row and queues a notification message.
  6. Follow it at /app/applications: status and the cell's latest note. A student may withdraw.

The officer's journey

  1. Sign in with an officer account. There is no public way to become an officer.
  2. Overview at /admin/dashboard: how many students, how many placed, open drives, applications.
  3. Companies and drives: add a recruiter, announce a drive with role, package, location, minimum CGPA, backlog limit, branches and dates. Every field is validated on the server.
  4. Pipeline at /admin/applications: filter by drive and status, move a candidate on, write a note the student will see.
  5. Shortlist at /admin/shortlist: tick many candidates, then commit them together. A stateful bean holds the selection between clicks.
  6. Reports, Database, Audit: Yuvraj and Chandra explain these.

How the applications were made, in case you are asked

Six are written by hand to give the demo accounts a story. The rest come from simple arithmetic over the student and drive lists, and only for pairs where the student really meets the drive's rules, using the same three tests as the live app. Nothing is random, so the data is the same every time and the demo can be rehearsed. Every date is counted from today.

Because dates are counted from the day the data was created, a database seeded last week has drives whose deadline has passed, and students cannot apply. Before the presentation run .\run.ps1 -Reseed. It empties the tables and lets the seeder fill them again with fresh dates.

7. Questions you will be asked

Who exactly uses this app?

Two kinds of people. Students, who register themselves, and placement officers, who are created by the college. Companies do not log in: the officer keeps their details and posts their drives, as a real placement cell does.

How does data get into the system?

Through forms. A student registration creates the account and profile. A student applying creates an application. An officer creating a company or a drive creates those rows. An officer can also import many students at once. Everything passes server side validation before it reaches the database, and the demo data is loaded once by the seeder.

How does data get out?

Students see a board of drives with their eligibility and their own applications. Officers see totals, a pipeline, reports read with JDBC, a browser for the database itself, and an audit trail. A student can also download their own resume.

Why can an officer not register on the public page?

Because the register form only ever creates a student. An officer account is a privilege, so it is created by the college. Only the seeder creates officers here. That keeps a student from choosing their own role.

Why are there no recruiter logins?

It was a scope decision. The cell is the go-between, so the officer posts drives for the company. A recruiter role would be a natural extension: a third role, its own pages, and an approval step by the officer.

A student says a drive is missing. Why?

The default board shows open drives. The board also has an "all" view. A drive whose last date has passed shows as not eligible with the reason "the last date to apply was ...", so nothing is hidden without explanation.

Is the eligibility check only on the page?

No. The page hides the button, and the servlet checks the rules again on the POST, because a page can be stale and a request can be hand written.

Where does the demo data come from, and is it real?

It is made up. The seeder creates it on the first start if the database is empty. It is deterministic, so it is the same every time, and the dates are counted from today so the drives are always current.

What did you not build?

Recruiter logins, email or SMS delivery of the notifications (a message is queued through JMS and handled by a bean, not sent by email), password reset by email, and confirmation links for new accounts. Two smaller gaps: nothing stops a student who already holds an offer from applying to more drives (the dashboard says the cell will be in touch about the policy), and the audit trail records the actor as "portal" instead of the individual officer, because sign in is done by the app's own filter rather than a server security realm. These are the honest next steps.

Which technologies are used and why?

Servlets and JSP for the web layer, EJB for business rules and transactions, JPA with Hibernate for ordinary reads and writes, JDBC for reports where SQL fits better than objects, MySQL as the database, GlassFish as the Jakarta EE server. Each one is a topic from the course.
